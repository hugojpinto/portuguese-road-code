How the model works, and its limits

  • Geometry: a four-arm roundabout about 64 m across, with two circulating lanes (centre-lines at 26 m and 30 m radius), two-lane entries and two-lane exits. Traffic circulates counter-clockwise.
  • Who is in which lane is decided by geometry. A car cutting across the outer lane is "in" the outer lane exactly while its body is there, so outer-lane drivers see it and brake (after their reaction time). Collisions are detected from the actual car footprints. No conflict type is scripted.
  • Giving way: entering cars and cars leaving from the inner lane use a conflict-zone gap test, re-checked continuously until they can no longer stop. Drivers can miss a car (more often in the blind spot) and can misread signals. Under PT 2014 they may also assume an outer-lane car will exit because the rule says it must.
  • Same drivers, same human-error rates, same random traffic for every rule. Only lane choice, in-ring behaviour, and what drivers expect of others change.
  • Absolute crash rates are inflated so crashes are frequent enough to compare. Only the ratios between rules mean anything.
  • Error-free check: with every human error switched off, lane integrity is almost crash-free. PT 2014 still produces some crashes. Most happen where a car moving out to the outer lane (as the rule requires) meets entrants from the arm it has just passed. This is a structural feature of the rule on this geometry, not driver error.
  • Not modelled: pedestrians, cyclists, spiral or turbo lane markings (physical lane guidance), and heavy vehicles straddling lanes. Crashed cars are removed instantly, which slightly relieves congestion.
