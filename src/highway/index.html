How the model works, and its limits

  • Car following: Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) with a 0.3–0.6 s reaction delay and anticipation. A fully attentive IDM driver never crashes, so every crash comes from a modelled human error: short and long distractions, a missed car when checking a gap (worst in the blind spot), and stress.
  • Lane changes: MOBIL model (incentive plus a safety check). Each rule set only changes the lane-choice incentives, whether passing on the right is allowed, and when tailgating and flashing are "licensed". Drivers, traffic and human-error rates are identical across rules: each replicate uses the same random traffic for every rule (common random numbers).
  • Absolute crash rates are deliberately inflated, by orders of magnitude, so crashes are frequent enough to compare. Only the ratios between rule sets mean anything. The error rates are calibrated so that, under the EU rule at medium-heavy traffic, rear-end crashes are roughly 40–50% of multi-vehicle crashes. That is close to real motorway data. If you push the error sliders, you can make either crash type dominate.
  • Not modelled: single-vehicle crashes (run-off-road, about 30% of real motorway crashes), weather, night-time, motorbikes, secondary crashes (crashed cars are removed instantly), and the slow cultural adaptation of drivers to a new rule.
  • Real-world reference: lane-change and sideswipe crashes are typically 10–25% of motorway crashes, and rear-end the largest category. The simulator is better at answering "which rule exposes drivers to more risky manoeuvres and stress" than "what share of crashes are lane changes".
